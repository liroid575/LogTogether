# v0.12.0 — Family-test reliability and clarity

This release turns the latest family test findings into one compatibility-preserving update. App state remains schema v1. Exact hydration and supplement entries, body metrics, local photos, and GPX routes keep their existing privacy boundaries.

## Timer and exercise records

- Timed hangs, static stretches, balance holds, intervals, carries, mobility, yoga, cardio, swimming, rounds, and skill work now start a countdown from the configured duration. An optional work target still overrides that duration.
- Completion stores actual active seconds separately from the planned target. History and analysis therefore do not silently replace a 22-second effort with a planned 60 seconds.
- Number fields accept valid whole values such as a 60-second jump-rope interval and 250 mL water. Forms still apply explicit bounds.
- Regular workout actions use larger full-width Start and Skip controls on narrow screens.

## Navigation and editing

- `Log completed activity` explains that it records something already finished and points people starting now to `Start workout`.
- `Beginner's guide` replaces `Quick Guide` and adds the monthly badge and completed-month hydration-border preview before the feedback page.
- Circuit and exercise creation are collapsible controls inside Start workout, with circuit first.
- Saved routines remain quick to reach on Home but are collapsed by default. Start workout now provides a detailed template editor, exercise/set reordering and removal, and batch deletion. Completed history is never modified by template edits.
- Hydration and supplements share a visible date/time control for backfilling. Both calendars explain their markers. Water retains the standard 2,000 mL default and its Settings explanation.

## Motivation and family comparison

- Weekly missions are a collapsed list. Each item expands to explain its rule, and health guidance explicitly says it has no mission points.
- Personal activity counts distinct active days. Choosing `Any activity` now means any recorded exercise or positive-duration hike rather than a small hard-coded subset.
- Weekly difficulty and chosen activity are snapshotted by week, so later changes do not rewrite older mission results.
- Family comparison uses the current user's local workouts for their own chart, labels the week, and shares only the chosen difficulty level alongside existing bounded weekly totals.
- History's top summary is general activity: active days, completed sessions, and active minutes.
- Gold Day thresholds remain intentionally approachable: 4 valid strength sets, 20 active cardio minutes, or 10 mobility/balance minutes, with mixed activity combining. Gold is a game completion signal, not a clinical prescription.

## Poke correction safety

Gold reward state and wallet updates now happen in one Firestore transaction. Deleting the activity that made today Gold revokes that day's granted token. If it was already spent, a correction debt is settled by the next eligible token. Restoring the same day reconciles the same ledger entry and cannot mint repeated tokens. Legacy reward entries are not retroactively debited because they do not contain enough attribution data.

## Science interpretation

The Health reference counts only activity explicitly recorded as moderate or vigorous. In the app's five-point effort field, 3 is moderate and 4–5 is vigorous; vigorous minutes count twice toward moderate-equivalent minutes. Missing or easy intensity remains visible in history but is not guessed into the health total. The strength-day number remains a logging aid and is explicitly not proof that all major muscle groups were trained.

This follows the distinction between moderate and vigorous aerobic activity and the common two-for-one equivalence in the [WHO physical activity guidelines](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK566046/) and the [CDC intensity guide](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/measuring/index.html). Exercise variety and progressive, individualized resistance training remain more important than chasing one rigid formula; see the [ACSM 2026 resistance-training update](https://acsm.org/resistance-training-guidelines-update-2026/). Stretching remains represented as mobility activity rather than aerobic or strength work, consistent with the [American Heart Association flexibility overview](https://www.heart.org/en/healthy-living/exercise-and-physical-activity/fitness-basics/flexibility-exercise-stretching).
